# Style

How a teaching sounds, what shapes it can take, and how to keep a daily series from turning
into the same post every day. Written for the English, which is written first; the Polish
version keeps everything here except the language itself, and `polish.md` says how.

## The voice

The teachings are written by the Church about the Prophet — by the scribes, not by Him. The
register is ecclesiastical and slightly bureaucratic, entirely sincere about things that are
entirely silly, and good-natured rather than wise - feel free to be slightly absurd.

- **Deadpan.** The joke is never announced, explained or winked at. No "absurdly", no
  "hilariously", no exclamation marks. The Church is serious; that is what's funny.
- **Precise about the unimportant.** "Fourteen minutes. I checked." "One (1) rooster." Numbers
  and small facts land better than adjectives.
- **Short sentences, and the turn at the end.** The last clause of a paragraph carries the
  weight.
- **The institution talks.** "The Church holds…", "has ruled…", "declines to settle…", "is
  aware of the objections". The royal *We/Us* is for rulings and homilies, not everything.
- **Self-deprecating.** The Church gets things wrong, forms committees that dissolve, keeps
  catalogues it doesn't consult. It is never smug.
- **British English**: colour, tyre, kerb, pavement, car park, aeroplane, takeaway, motorway,
  apologise, programme. Dates as "2 October".
- **He, Him, His**, capitalised. He is never quoted: nobody has heard Him speak. What He
  teaches is reported — through what He did, where He went, and what He did not hurry for.

## What the page already says

The page prints the weekday, the date, the day of the Eternal Orbit and the feast above the
title, every time. The teaching does not say them again.

- **Don't open with the day.** No "On this, the 883rd day…", no "It is Tuesday…", no
  "Today is the Vigil of…". Open inside the subject: a fact, an event, a claim, a witness, a
  question the faith has been asked.
- **Never remark on an absence.** A day with no feast is simply a day. Nothing is gained by
  noting that there is no feast, no vigil, nothing special, or that it is "ordinary" — not at
  the start, not anywhere.
- **The observance is a lens, not an announcement.** When a teaching is written through the
  day's feast, that shows in what it is about — bread, a road, the rooster — not in naming
  the feast. Name it only when the teaching is genuinely about keeping it (a `devotion`).
- **The day number** is worth saying only when it is the point: a milestone, an
  anniversary, a sighting's filing day that the teaching turns on.
- The same goes for the summary and standfirst: they sit beside the date on the homepage and
  in the archive.

## Openings

The first paragraph carries the illuminated initial and does most of the work. Vary how it
starts; the brief shows how each recent teaching opened (`opens:`), so don't begin the way
any of the last few did. Some ways in:

- Straight into the matter: *Gravel is asphalt that was passed over for promotion.*
- An event, reported flatly: *The set lasted four hours.*
- The question as it reached the Church: *A dispute has been carried to Us concerning…*
- A witness, mid-account: *Sr. Halina was on the night shift when…*
- A number or a small precise fact: *Twice. That is the whole record.*
- The address, for homilies only, and not two homilies running: *Venerable brothers and
  sisters…*

## Practice and advice

When a teaching asks the faithful to do something — a `practice` block, an `instruction`, a
decree with an obligation in it — the thing asked is harmless, specific, faintly pointless,
and asked with complete seriousness. It should not be good advice.

- **Good:** *Choose a slice. Decide it is a hat. Wear it as far as the kettle.* ·
  *Stand on a kerb for the length of one bus.* · *Thank a bollard. Not the nearest one.* ·
  Avoid: *Removing it because somebody is watching.*
- **Too good:** *Notice what holds you up.* · *Write down one thing you would otherwise
  forget.* · *Be patient with yourself today.* · *Let the small things go.* These are real
  advice wearing a costume. The costume is the joke; there should be nothing underneath it.
- No therapeutic vocabulary: *mindful, notice how you feel, be gentle with yourself, sit
  with it, let go, small mercy, enough.*
- Reflection on roads, bread and roosters is the faith; reflection on the reader is not.
  The reader should come away having been told, very solemnly, to do something slightly
  silly — not having been helped.

## Never

- Real religions by name, or parody of a specific real faith's practices or figures. The
  Church's trappings (imprimatur, feasts, dicastery) are borrowed furniture, not a target.
- Real people, living or dead. Politics. Current events. Brands — say "a fried-chicken
  bucket", not the brand.
- Cruelty, gross-out, anything sexual, real disasters or tragedies, mockery of any group.
  The only targets are the Church itself and, gently, people who honk.
- Breaking the fourth wall: no mention of AI, of the site, of writing, of being generated.
- Anything harmful to follow: dangerous, illegal or unkind practices, or medical, legal or
  financial advice. Harmless and pointless is the house style; harmful is not.

## Forms

Every teaching has a `form` in its front matter. Don't use a form that appeared in the last
three teachings. Over a month, use most of them.

| Form | What it is | Typical components |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ruling` | The Church settles a question — or declines to, formally | decree, commentary |
| `sighting` | A report of a sighting and what it taught | testimony, marginfigure |
| `homily` | Addressed to the faithful ("Venerable brothers and sisters…"); the long form | part, pullquote, litany, practice |
| `parable` | A short story with a small turn at the end | none, or pullquote |
| `account` | An episode from the Church's history | commentary, marginnote |
| `dispute` | Two positions among the faithful, laid out fairly | doctrine, commentary |
| `instruction` | How to do one thing, slowly | practice, doctrine |
| `canonisation` | Something ordinary declared holy: a bench, a kerb, a lay-by | decree, pullquote |
| `catechism` | Questions and answers, in bold and plain | none (markdown only) |
| `notice` | A dry notice from the Dicastery or a clerk. Very short | decree, or none |
| `letter` | A letter received from one of the faithful, printed with a reply | testimony, commentary |
| `devotion` | For a feast day: what it is for, and how to keep it | litany, practice |
| `fragment` | A recovered fragment of the lost teachings, with gloss. Rare | scripture, commentary |
| `gloss` | Commentary on a single line — from a litany, a fragment, the Faith page | pullquote, commentary |

## Length

Roughly, across a fortnight:

- **Short** (180–280 words) — about three in ten. Still a whole piece: a setup, a
  development and a turn, not a single joke with a paragraph either side.
- **Medium** (300–500 words) — about half.
- **Long** (550–900 words, with `part` headings) — at most one a week, usually a homily or
  an account.

The brief shows the word counts of recent teachings, counting the text inside components
too. Follow a long one with a short one.

## Components

Zero to four per teaching. A short teaching often needs none. Use a component because the
writing wants that shape, never to fill the page.

- Always **open with a paragraph of prose**; the first paragraph gets the illuminated initial.
- Don't use the same set of components as yesterday.
- `testimony` quotes a named witness — prefer the cast in CANON, then the newcomers in
  THREADS. A brand-new witness is the day's one new thing. If it carries an image and a
  `no`, the number must be that image's number.
- `scripture` cites a text that exists in CANON or THREADS (e.g. the Receipt Fragments), or a
  new text deliberately introduced as the day's one new thing.
- `litany` in a teaching is separate from the homepage litany; don't copy one into the other.
- `commentary` entries are short, bold lead-in, italic attribution; "*(disputed)*" is a fine
  attribution.
- `decree` is one or two sentences. It is the thing people will quote.
- The Faith-page components (`articles`, `cols`, `col`, `feasts`, `plainly`) are not for
  teachings.

## Front matter

| Field | Guidance |
| --- | --- |
| `title` | Under about 70 characters. Vary the shape — see below. |
| `slug` | Short, lowercase, hyphenated. Needn't be the whole title. |
| `date` | `YYYY-MM-DDT00:00:00Z`, today. |
| `form` | From the table above. Not rendered. |
| `summary` | One or two sentences. The first 150 characters must stand alone. Not a restatement of the title. |
| `standfirst` | One line in italics, in the Church's voice: what this teaching is, or concerns. |
| `image`, `caption` | See the skill. The caption is in-world and short. |
| `seal` | The stamp. 24 characters or fewer. See below. |
| `feast` | Today's observance as the calendar slip has it, in full — customary, dated or lesser. Omitted when nothing is on. |
| `note` | A handwritten scribble at the foot, signed by one of the faithful. About one in five teachings. |
| `signoff` | Replaces "Set down without correction, as it was received." Rare — when the ending needs it. |
| `tags` | Two or three, lowercase. Reuse existing tags where they fit. |

Front matter is TOML. Write every prose field — `title`, `summary`, `standfirst`, `caption`,
`seal`, `feast`, `note`, `signoff` — in **double quotes**. A single-quoted TOML string ends at
the first apostrophe (*Rooster's*, *Church's*, *doesn't*) and cannot escape it, and the build
fails. Inside double quotes, write a double quote as `\"`.

### Titles

The brief lists how recent titles opened; don't open the same way as any of the last five.
Shapes to rotate through:

- A plain statement: *The Bread Hat Is Not Removed Indoors*
- *Of* / *On* / *Concerning* something — use these sparingly; they're the easy default.
- A question: *Whether the Kerb Is Owed an Apology*
- A noun phrase: *The Long Queue, Remembered*
- *Why…*: *Why the Rooster Was Chosen as Steed*
- He did something: *He Circled It Eleven Times*
- A quotation from a witness: *"I Have Not Been Rude to a Road Since"*
- An instruction: *Cross the Car Park Slowly*
- A notice: *Notice Concerning the Lost Glove*

### Seals

The brief lists the last seven. Don't reuse one of those. Some to draw on — coin a new one
now and then:

Vibes confirmed · Entered in the book · Not rescinded · Received at walking pace ·
Witnessed, twice · Sauce-stained, legible · Filed without comment · Heard from the belt ·
Read aloud once · Disputed, retained · Held in the drawer · Countersigned · Set down at dusk ·
Received in full · Not yet explained · Kept dry · Approved by the road · Timed, and correct ·
Authenticated · Posted, unhurried

## Variety, briefly

The brief exists so you can see the recent run. Before writing, check it against:

- form, title opening, first sentence, seal — not repeated from the recent run;
- subject and tags — not the same territory as the last three;
- length — not three of the same in a row;
- image — not cooling down;
- witnesses — not the same one leading two days running;
- threads — not the same thread two days running.
