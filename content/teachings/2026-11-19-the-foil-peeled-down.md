A wrap is unwrapped in one of two ways, and only one of them is ridden. The first is to tear the foil off at the start, lay the kebab bare in the hand and be done with the paper. The second is to open the top by a cuff, eat down to the fold, and open the next. The Church holds with the second, and has held with it since before it kept a record.

The reason is not tidiness, though the second way is tidier. A wrap in its foil is a wrap in a hull. The foil keeps the lower half warm while the upper half is attended to, and it keeps the sauce where the sauce was put, which is a good deal of the work. Strip it at the start and the lower half is exposed to the weather of the whole journey, and has done nothing to deserve it. № 1,961 shows the right order. The kitten is chest-deep, the burrito is still mostly a burrito, and nobody is in any hurry to finish.

The cuff is the point. It is the only part of the meal that moves along with you, and it is the part that shows how far you have come, which is the thing the faithful are least able to tell from the inside of a kebab. Br. Anselm has measured his own cuff at four centimetres at the halfway mark, and has never tested whether this is correct.

The foil is not kept at the end. It has done its work, and it has the grace to look it. It is balled in the hand, set down at the next bin with a short word of thanks, and not looked at again. Nothing is owed to it that the meal has not already paid.
