The napkin was not a good napkin. It was thin, single-ply and already creased, and the first draft of the first teaching was set down on it in a pen that skipped. The Church wishes this known before anyone asks to see it.

The draft ran to eleven lines. Four were crossed out, one was crossed out and then restored, and one was crossed out so thoroughly that the napkin gave way. What remained was carried into the Book as the opening teaching, with the napkin tucked behind it, until a meal arrived and the napkin was used for what napkins are for. The clerks hold that this was the right order of events. A draft that has been wiped upon has been, in a sense, read.

It survives, folded, in the drawer beside the one that holds the unphotographed sightings. It has a ring of sauce near the top, which the Church has declined to read as punctuation.

{{% decree %}}
The Napkin Draft is kept, and is not unfolded. Anyone who wishes to know what it says is referred to the Book, which says it better.
{{% /decree %}}

Notice is further given that napkins in general are not to be held in contempt on account of this one. Most of them never wrote anything.
