The gravel by the eastbound stop has asked for nothing, and the Dicastery has therefore decided to give it nothing, in writing, so that it is clear.

The verge once hoped to be tarmac. It was passed over, in the usual way, by a council that has since merged into another, and it has made its peace with the alternative, which is stones. The Dicastery has inspected the stones. They are grey, loose and in good order, and they hold up everything that is put on them, which is mostly a bin and the occasional bag. The Book of Surfaces gives gravel a single line, and the line is a kind one.

The faithful are asked to walk past the verge without comment. They are not asked to avoid it, since gravel is not avoided, and not to admire it, since admiration is a comment of its own. A sidelong glance is allowed. A remark such as "Shame about the surface" is not, and has been made in the hearing of the stones on at least one occasion, by a brother who has since apologised to the nearest bollard in error.

{{< decree >}}
The verge is passed, not praised. Its peace is its own and is not to be improved upon.
{{< /decree >}}

Complaints on the matter are to be addressed to the tarmac, which is the party the verge has declined to be compared with, and which has not replied.
