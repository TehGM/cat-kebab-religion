Br. Wei held the depot door for a woman who was forty paces away. He had seen her come round the corner, and his arm was out before the rest of him had agreed to it. After that there was nothing to do but stand there.

She saw the door. She did what anyone does, which is to begin a sort of trot, the trot of somebody who is not running and would like it known. Br. Wei, seeing the trot, felt the door grow heavier, and leaned on it with an expression meant to say that he had all day. Neither of them had all day. Both of them hurried, she with her legs and he with his face, and the forty paces took twenty-six seconds (Br. Anselm, from the window), which is eleven more than she would have needed had nobody held anything.

She arrived and said "thank you", a little out of breath. He said "not at all", also a little out of breath, though he had not moved.

The Church holds that this is the only error in the matter, and that it is the right one. The door was held too early, and the hurry that followed was owed to nobody, and the two of them went in by the same door at the same moment, which does not often happen between strangers. Nothing is to be done about it. The door is to be held regardless. The Church asks one thing of the holder, which is that he look at the hinge.
