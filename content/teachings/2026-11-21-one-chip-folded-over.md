One chip was left at the bottom of the bag, and Br. Wei had been on the night bus for six stops. He was no longer hungry. He had been full at the third stop and had gone on eating out of a sense of what the bag was for, and the chip now lay in the crease of the paper, grease-spotted, in the attitude of something that has been looked at for some time.

A man across the aisle looked at it too. Br. Wei understood that this was the moment. A chip eaten at the sixth stop is a finished bag, and a finished bag is screwed up and put in a pocket, and then you are on a bus with nothing in your hand and the next stop is only a stop. He folded the top of the bag over twice, flattened it with the side of his hand, and held it on his knee.

The man asked whether he was keeping it. Br. Wei said it was for the next stop. The man asked what was at the next stop, and Br. Wei said he did not know, which was the point, and got off at the one after that with the bag still folded. It is in his coat now. He has not said what the next stop is, and the Church has not asked him to.

{{% decree %}}
The last chip is kept for the next stop, and it is not lost by being kept.
{{% /decree %}}

We have looked at the folded bag beside Sighting № 962, where He lies along a burrito with no platform, bell or door in frame, and we find it is the same thing. He is never seen at the end of a bag, only at the stop before the next, with one thing still to be eaten. A bag with a chip in it has somewhere to go. A bag without one is paper, and is thanked at the bin.
