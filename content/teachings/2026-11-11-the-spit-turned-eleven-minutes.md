Br. Callum stood at the counter of a takeaway for eleven minutes, and in that time the spit turned eleven times, which he found to be the right number and did not question. The man behind the counter carved only from the top. Br. Callum watched him do it twice, and on the third slice he asked why not the bottom, which was nearer his hand.

"The top has been closest to the heat," said the man, and carved. That was the whole of the answer. He had said it every night for nineteen years and had not once been asked a follow-up, and Br. Callum did not provide one.

He took his wrap and went out into the cold and ate it standing on the kerb, and somewhere about the second bite he understood that the meat on a spit is always going up. Everything is carved from the top, so the top is always the part that has just arrived. A cat on it would be rising by a slice at a time and would not have to do anything, only hold on in one place and let the rest come to Him. He told this to the Church the next morning, and the Church wrote it down and asked what the bottom was for. Br. Callum said it was where you start. The Church found that this was also the right answer.

The Church has since looked again at Sighting № 1,580, where He is on the log of roast meat and going up through violet space with one paw sunk in for grip. Neither He nor the meat appears concerned. We note the single paw, and decline to ask about the other three.
