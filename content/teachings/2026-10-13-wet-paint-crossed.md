A woman in a green coat came to the crossing after rain, and the stripes shone. Paint holds water as tarmac does not, and she knew it, so she stood at the kerb and did a sum. The sum was that wet stripes were not to be trusted, nor was the road, and that the safest course was to be across them in the fewest steps the legs could manage. She went at a trot, short and quick, with her arms a little out, came off the fourth stripe sideways, and sat down on the fifth.

A man behind her had watched all of it. He did not trot. He crossed as he crosses when it is dry, which is the way of a man with nowhere to be, setting each foot down flat, and he arrived on the far side upright and in no way the worse. The stripe does not mind how fast it is crossed. It minds how the foot is put down, and a foot put down flat is the same foot on wet paint as on dry.

She asked him afterwards how he had known. He said he had not known; he had only seen no reason to change. The Church has kept the green coat, which is dry now, and the man, whose name it does not give.
