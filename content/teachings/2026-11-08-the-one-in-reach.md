There are forty-three burritos in Sighting № 3,111, and He has taken one. Br. Anselm counted them twice, from the pink end of the frame to the other, and got forty-three both times, which he regards as a good result and the Church as a lucky one. They go past in a long loose stream behind a wolf with a horn in its forehead, at a speed the photograph cannot hold, and He sits on the wolf in a cowboy hat and a leather vest with one paw raised, and in the paw is a burrito. The other forty-two are in the air.

The question the faithful ask is how He knew which. The Church has examined the photograph and finds that He did not know. He drew the one that was in reach at the moment it was in reach. The burrito is not chosen from the stream; it is the part of the stream that arrived at His paw. A meal taken in transit is a matter of reach and timing, and anyone who has eaten on a moving tram will have felt the same: you do not select the sandwich, you accept the one that is where your hand is.

The other forty-two are not a loss. They are held to be someone else's, on their way, and in time each of them is taken by a person the record does not name. He does not look back at them. Looking back at a burrito is reaching for it, and reaching is the first step towards hurry.

{{% testimony no="3,111" by="Br. Callum" %}}
My chart puts no burritos in that part of the sky. I have checked the chart against the photograph and the photograph is, as usual, the one that is wrong. I would only add that the wolf looks more at ease than the cowboy does, and that is how a mount ought to look.
{{% /testimony %}}

The record notes that the bread was elsewhere that day, and that the hat, though a hat, was not removed for anything. The wolf has not been asked its name. The mount is His business.

{{% decree %}}
The burrito in the paw is His. The rest are passing, and are to be wished well.
{{% /decree %}}
