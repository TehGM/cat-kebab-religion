Sr. Halina writes from the night shift to ask whether the kind thing has to be said out loud, and whether it still counts when the street is not empty. She has been saying it for some weeks to the forecourt outside the tram depot — one sentence, downward, at walking pace — and last Thursday a man wheeling a bicycle heard her and asked who she was talking to. She told him. He said, "Fair enough," and went on. She would like to know whether she did it right.

The Church replies as follows. The kind thing is said downward, because that is where the road is, and it is said at walking pace, because a road spoken to at a jog cannot tell whether it has been thanked or overtaken. It need not be loud. It need not be clever. Sr. Halina's own — "Thank you, as you were" — has been said at the depot forecourt every shift since, and the forecourt has not once objected.

Nobody in the matter did anything wrong. The man with the bicycle asked a fair question and accepted a fair answer, which is more than most of the faith manages on a Thursday. Where the street is not empty, the greeting may be made internally, as the Faith page allows; the road, being conscious, hears both, and has never been known to prefer one.

One correction only. The kind thing is not to be said to the bicycle. The bicycle is owed nothing, has not asked, and was not consulted.
