The heel sat in the depot canteen's bread bin for nine days, and in that time eleven people took the slice beneath it. Each of them saw it. Each lifted the other slices round it with a delicacy the Church would like to see applied elsewhere, and left it for the next person, who was bound to want it more.

This is the courtesy of the end slice, and it is the oldest deadlock in the canteen. Nobody dislikes the heel. It is brown, it has crust on both sides of it, and it is the only slice in the loaf that has been in the oven the whole time. But to take it is to say that you chose it, and nobody did. One was simply last.

Br. Anselm kept the count, for reasons he has not given. On the third morning the heel had been passed over four times, on the sixth seven, and by the ninth it had developed a certain dignity, the kind a slice gets from being walked round.

Sr. Maeve ended it on the tenth morning. She did not eat it at the bin. She did not toast it, which would have improved it, and which the Church has since ruled would therefore have been a mistake. She put it on her head, stood still in the canteen for the length of one kettle coming to the boil, and carried it to the kettle. Then she took it off, buttered it, and ate it sitting down.

{{% decree %}}
The heel is kept for the hat. Anyone may take it, and nobody is to be seen choosing it.
{{% /decree %}}

The next loaf's heel was gone by the first morning, taken by the first person in, and worn. Nobody said anything, and nobody needed to. The board it was cut on still has the notch.
