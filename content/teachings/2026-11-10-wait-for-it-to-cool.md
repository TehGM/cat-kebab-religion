A cushion He has just left is not empty. It holds, for a while, the exact shape of the last thing that was on it and a good deal of its temperature, and the Church counts both as His until they have gone. Nobody sits in it. Nobody has been told not to. The faithful simply arrive at the edge of the warm patch, look at it, and find that they would rather stand.

The vigil is kept like this. Stand beside the cushion, or sit on the arm of the chair with your hands in your lap, and wait for the warmth to stop. In a heated room this takes between nine minutes and a quarter of an hour (Br. Anselm, with the back of his hand; he asks that the method not be called a thermometer). It is not to be hurried. A hand laid flat on the cushion to see how it is getting on puts the vigil back by a minute each time, and Br. Anselm has put it back a long way.

A sister asked what is to be done if He comes back before the cushion has cooled. The Church holds that this is the best outcome there is, and cannot say why. He steps into the shape, turns once, and the vigil is over without having ended, which is permitted.

When it is cold, it is a cushion again. Someone plumps it, and it never lies quite as it did before, which the Church regards as correct.
