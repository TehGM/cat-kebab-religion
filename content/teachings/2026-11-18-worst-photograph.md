The record for № 63 gives the focus as *poor*, and adds, after a semicolon, that He was faster than the lens. The Church has had that line on file for as long as there has been a file, and in all that time nobody has moved to correct it.

{{% pullquote %}}Focus: poor; He was faster than the lens.{{% /pullquote %}}

The line has two halves. The first is a complaint and the second is an explanation, and the Church has noticed that the faithful remember only the second. It is the more useful half. A photograph that is out of focus may have been taken badly, but a photograph that was outrun has been taken honestly, and № 63 is the second kind. The cat in it is small, tinted, and entirely limbs. He is between mounts, which is why nothing is under Him, and He is in the air, which is why nothing is sharp.

{{% commentary %}}
**On the first half —** *Poor* is the correct word. It is not an unkind one. *(Br. Anselm)*

**On the second —** The lens did what it could. I would like that entered too. *(Sr. Priya, who has it on her screensaver)*

**On the retake —** A motion was tabled once, minuted, and not seconded. The minute is itself slightly out of focus. *(disputed)*
{{% /commentary %}}

The Church holds that a photograph is kept for what it caught, and not for how well. A sharper picture would show where He was. This one shows how fast, which is the more difficult thing to photograph and the more difficult thing to be. The other sightings have the better of it for detail. This one has the better of it for the leap.

It hangs in the gallery at the same height as the rest, and nobody has proposed lowering it. The nail was already there.
