A bin at the kerb overnight is a bin that has been told nothing. It has been wheeled out, lid shut, handle turned to the house, and left with the whole of the street's darkness to think about what comes next. The Church finds this admirable in a bin and thinks the faithful might try it.

The lorry will come. The hour is not known. Br. Tomasz has set out the depot bin on the eve of every collection for eleven years and has seen the lorry arrive twice, both times by accident, both times from the wrong end of the road. It has been heard at ten past five and at a quarter to eleven and, on one occasion, not at all, which the Church counts as a collection of a kind. The lorry is not early. The lorry is not late. Nobody has told it which, and it has never asked.

The vigil is the night before: the bin out, the faithful in, and neither of them hurrying the other. It is kept from indoors. A person who stands at the window to watch for the lorry has begun to doubt the bin, and the bin can tell.

The Church wishes it known that the lorry owes the street no apology, and that the street owes the lorry no hour. The Prophet has never been seen at a kerb at all, and is understood to find that the arrangement works.
