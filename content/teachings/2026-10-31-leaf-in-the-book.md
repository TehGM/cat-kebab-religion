A leaf has been found in the Book, between the pages for days 410 and 411, flat and brown and pressed. It was put there on purpose. The clerk has not been able to learn by whom.

The Church acknowledges the kindness. A leaf in a book is a dry leaf, and a dry leaf has simply been kept. The leaf the Church has in mind is the other kind: the one the rain has laid on the pavement, which is no longer a leaf but a part of the pavement, and is walked on as a part of the pavement is. Nobody lifted it. That is the whole of its holiness. Lifted, it would be a leaf again, and a leaf is a thing that must afterwards be put somewhere.

The faithful are asked not to bring wet leaves into the reading room. The one brought in on Tuesday has left a shape on the page for day 411 that the clerk reads as a hand and Br. Anselm reads as a map. Both readings are entered.

{{< decree >}}
A leaf that has become the pavement is left as the pavement. A leaf in the Book may stay, and is turned past, not lifted.
{{< /decree >}}

The leaf between days 410 and 411 remains where it was found. The Church has not looked to see what it is a leaf of.
