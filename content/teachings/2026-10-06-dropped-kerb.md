A kerb is a raised thing, and most of them hold the position for life. One, outside the tram depot, did not. It was lowered some years ago to the level of the road, in a small rectangle of ribbed paving, so that anyone with a pushchair, a trolley or a firm sense of direction could cross without first being asked to step up. It has been flush ever since. The council that lowered it has since been merged into another council, which does not know it is there.

Nobody has thanked it, and the Church can see why. It is at ankle height, and it is doing its job so well that the job has stopped being visible. Br. Anselm has measured the drop. It is nought point nine of a centimetre, which he calls "a lip, not a kerb", and which he has declined to round up to one.

The Church has considered what a kerb is for. A kerb is for being higher than the road, and every other kerb in the district does this faithfully and without complaint. This one gave the height away. It was not worn down, or knocked, or neglected into it. It was dropped on purpose, and has stayed dropped through four winters and one resurfacing, and the Church holds that this is the nearest a piece of pavement can come to a vocation.

{{% pullquote %}}It did not fall. It went down on purpose, and was walked over for it.{{% /pullquote %}}

There is also the matter of Sighting № 1,660, in which He comes in low toward a green moon, head-down, at some speed. The Church does not say the two are related. It notes that nobody has said they are not.

{{% decree %}}
The dropped kerb outside the tram depot is canonised at its present height, which is its whole merit. It is not to be raised, repainted, or stepped over on purpose.
{{% /decree %}}

The kerb has not been told. It is understood that it would not mind, and that if it did, it would say nothing, and would remain level.
