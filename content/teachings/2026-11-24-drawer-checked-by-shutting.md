The clerk checked the drawer at nine, in the only way the Church allows, which is by closing it. It went in as far as it goes and stopped with the small flat sound that the log records as *shut*. It held what it has always held: thirty-one unposted letters, a second skewer, a folded napkin draft that is not unfolded, and the sightings that have never been photographed. None of these was looked at. The drawer was closed upon them, and they were found to be inside.

Each year the Church is asked whether the drawer ought not to be opened, just once, to see how it is getting on. It ought not. A drawer opened to be checked is a drawer in use, and a drawer in use must be shut again afterwards, which is the same check with an extra step and a worse mood. The test of this drawer is that it shuts. It has done so every year, the runner catching at the third inch and then giving way, and the Church takes the giving way to be the drawer's answer.

Notice is therefore given that the drawer is in order. Faithful with business in it are asked to bring the business another day. Faithful who have put something in it, and wish to know whether it is still there, are referred to the sound.
