He is stroked, and then He washes the place. The faithful have taken this to heart for as long as there have been hands, and the Dicastery of Vibes receives on average one letter a month asking whether it was a comment.

It was not. The Dicastery has looked into the shoulder, which is where it is most often taken, and finds that the washing starts there only because that is where the hand was. It ends at the paw because the paw is where the shoulder leads. Br. Callum timed one in the hall: four seconds of hand, nine of washing, and a pause of two in which He appeared to regard the matter as closed. He was already facing the way He was going.

That is the whole of it. A cat that has been stroked has been somewhere, and is tidied for the next place. The hand was a stop, a good one, and nobody washes at a stop they disliked; they simply leave. The washing is the Church's best evidence that the hand was admitted.

The Dicastery asks that no further letters be sent on the shoulder. Letters on the other shoulder will be read.

{{% decree %}}
A hand that has been washed after is still a hand that was admitted. It is held out again at the same height, and not looked at.
{{% /decree %}}
