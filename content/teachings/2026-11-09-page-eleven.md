Br. Okonkwo had got as far as seven across when He lay down on page eleven. The paper was open on the kitchen table, the pen was uncapped, and He arrived from the direction of the window, did not look at the crossword, and settled on it like one who had been on the page since it was printed. Eight clues remained. All eight were under His chest.

Br. Okonkwo waited eleven minutes. He then read the clues that were showing, guessed the rest from the edges, and wrote to the Church to ask whether the page was now His or was still being read.

{{% commentary %}}
**On the waiting —** Eleven minutes is not long for a page. It is long for a pen. *(Br. Okonkwo)*

**On what was underneath —** Four down, probably, and a stain. *(disputed)*

**On the pen —** It was not used on Him. I would like that entered. *(Br. Okonkwo, who knows it is not a ruling)*
{{% /commentary %}}

The Church has weighed it. A page that is read is a page in use, and a page that is sat on is also in use, and the Church sees no cause to rank the two. He did not take the crossword. He passed through it, as He passes through a kitchen, a hall, and a cardigan, and the paper was warm when He went. The reader may not demand the page back. The reader may only read what He has left showing, which is a great deal more than the reader supposes.

Seven across, Br. Okonkwo reports, was a word of five letters meaning *patience*. He has since written in *sleep*. The Church has not checked it and will not.

{{% decree %}}
A page that is sat on is read around. What is underneath is guessed, or left, and neither is a failure.
{{% /decree %}}
