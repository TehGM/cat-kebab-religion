The shelter at the eastbound stop has three walls, one bench and a roof that lets in a single steady drip over the second seat. It has never been cleaned to the Church's knowledge, and it has never been closed. For as long as there have been faithful at that stop, they have gone into it when the rain came, and stood in it until the rain left, and come out into a brighter road than they went in to.

The Church has now looked at it properly. The bench seats three, and has been sat on by five. The timetable behind the scratched panel gives the buses of a year nobody can name, and a bus has come regardless, every time, a little after the faithful had given up on it. The shower always ends while the shelter is still full, and the sky, on its way out, puts something curved over the car park that nobody has asked for.

The faithful look at it from under the roof, and none of them goes out to meet it. It keeps its distance, as it always has. The shelter is the correct place to be when that happens: dry enough, and not yet moving.

{{% decree %}}
The shelter at the eastbound stop is canonised as it stands: three walls, one bench, one drip. The drip is not to be mended. It is the shelter's only remark.
{{% /decree %}}

A brother once put a bucket under the drip, and it filled in forty minutes, and he came back to find it had been emptied by somebody who left no note. It has not been moved since. The Church counts it as the shelter's own, like the bench and the wall, and requires that nobody thank it aloud, in case it has views.

The canonisation takes effect at once, and does not require that anyone be waiting for a bus. It is enough to be under the roof when it is raining on it, and to stay a little after, until the sky has finished.
