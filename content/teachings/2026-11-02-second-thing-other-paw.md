A taco in the left paw, a slice in the right, and the tongue free to go between them. That is the whole posture, and the Faith page asks for it in a single line: kebab, in either paw, and preferably in both. The Church has since found that two paws are easy to fill and hard to be fair to.

The difficulty is the middle. A person holding two meals will, within a minute, begin to prefer one. The preferred one is lifted higher, turned towards the light, and licked first. The other is held out to the side like a coat for someone else. Sighting № 3,033 shows none of this. The taco and the slice are level, the tongue is out for both, and the Church has looked at the photograph for some years without finding the moment at which He chose.

He did not choose. Sr. Priya, who has counted, reports forty-one licks, alternating, and a forty-second that went to the nebula.

Nor is either meal put down in order to manage the other. A meal put down is a meal put on a surface, and the surfaces have been through enough. When a third thing arrives, such as a napkin, it is not taken by a paw. It is accepted by a knee, and the faithful who have tried a third paw have been reminded, gently, that there are only two.

The Church does not know what He prefers. It has stopped asking, on the grounds that the question was always going to be answered with a lick.
