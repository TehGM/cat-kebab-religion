Low numbers are supposed to mean early, and for most of the wall that holds up well enough. Not for № 4. The Stack — a kitten balanced on a llama balanced on a turtle balanced on a burrito, four tiers under one modest rainbow, every last one of them calm about the arrangement — carries one of the lowest numbers in the whole record, and nothing beneath that number says when the photograph was actually taken. It was simply found first, by whoever first went looking, and given whichever number came next.

{{% marginnote note="tried it once. put it back by teatime. — Br. Tomasz" %}}
Most of what the Church holds was never photographed at all — logged on faith alone, kept in the drawer, and numbered in the order it turned up rather than the order it happened. № 4,118 is recent and knows it. № 4 does not, and cannot be made to.
{{% /marginnote %}}

This is not, on reflection, a fault so much as a second record sitting on top of the first: the sightings themselves, and separately, quite unrelated to them, the order in which the Church happened to notice. Nobody has proposed correcting it, on the reasonable grounds that correcting it would only swap one arbitrary order for a more embarrassing one, since nobody was keeping receipts on the receipts either.

What can be said for № 4, plainly, is this: it was found early, it has never once been disputed, and the four of them — kitten, llama, turtle, burrito — remain the most relaxed the faith has ever looked about its own paperwork.
