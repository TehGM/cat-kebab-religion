Nobody taught the rooster to fly. He goes through open space regardless, at the same unhurried clip as everything else He has been seen upon, on some principle the rooster has never been made to explain, and has never once been asked to. The Church tried, briefly, in the early days, and got nothing back but a look. Explaining it was never the point of the Feast; being grateful for it, on the record, once, is.

{{< litany label="Said standing, sauce in hand" >}}
Flightless still | and carries Him regardless.
Colleague, not food | we have said so, and meant it.
Never once late | not even flightless.
{{< /litany >}}

What the Feast asks of the faithful is small, and has been misunderstood exactly as often as it has been kept. It is not an invitation. At least once, somebody has arrived with an actual rooster, in the sincere belief that this was the honouring part. It was not. The honouring part is the sauce.

{{< practice
  do="Bring the good sauce. Bring enough for two, on the chance He's hungry and the rooster isn't."
  avoid="Bringing the rooster. He is already here, and working." >}}

He does not attend His own Feast, strictly speaking — He is too busy being carried through it, flightless and on time, which was rather the point being made. That, the Church holds, is closer to honouring a colleague than anything said about him would be.
