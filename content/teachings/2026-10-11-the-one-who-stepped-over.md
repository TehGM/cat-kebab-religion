Br. Callum was late for a tram, and the dropped kerb outside the depot was in his way, so he stepped over it. It was one long stride. He cleared the lip, which is nought point nine of a centimetre (Br. Anselm, unrounded), by something under a hand, and landed on the far side with the look of a man who has saved four seconds.

The kerb did nothing. This is the whole of what it did, and the Church has since come to regard it as the point. A kerb that has given up its height for the sake of others does not object when one of them declines the gift. It lay level, as before, and the tram was already gone.

Callum stood on the road a moment. Then he walked back across the kerb, slowly, as far as the lip, and crossed it again properly, and then stood on the pavement for the length of one (1) bus. He has told the Church that nothing happened, and that he felt looked at by something that was not looking.

The Church has not made a rule of it. The feast keeps the kerb level and records that it is not stepped over, but the Church has been careful to say that this is a kindness rather than a prohibition, since a prohibition would only give the kerb something to enforce. What the faithful do is cross it. They do it at the pace of the person ahead, and they do it without a stride that could be mistaken for a leap.

{{% marginnote note="the council wrote to it in the spring. 'The Occupier of the Kerb.' it did not reply. — Br. Tomasz" %}}
The successor council, which does not know the kerb is there, sends it a letter once a year. The Church forwards these to the depot, where they are kept.
{{% /marginnote %}}

Callum caught the next tram. It was four minutes later, and it was the same tram.
