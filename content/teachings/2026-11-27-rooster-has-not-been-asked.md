**Does the rooster fly?**

It does not. It has been seen to carry Him across open space and over the ring road in a storm, with the wings out and held still, and the Church has never once seen them move. It is not a flight. It is a carrying.

**Then how does it carry Him?**

The Church asked, once, in the early days, and was given a look. The look has been kept. It is not a refusal and it is not an answer, and it ran to about four seconds, which the Church thinks is generous for a bird.

**Is He steering?**

He is holding a kebab aloft in one paw, and there is nothing in the other that could be a rein. The shield at the rooster's side is no help; it is a different question, and one the Church has not yet put aloud. The route is the rooster's, or the road's, or nobody's. All three have been proposed, and none of them has objected.

**Should the rooster be asked?**

No. The faithful are asked to notice that a mount which has never been questioned has never once been late, and that the question is therefore not one the Church can afford. Br. Callum has drafted it three times. The drafts are in the drawer, folded, beside the other things that were nearly sent.

**May the rooster be thanked, then?**

It may, and from below, and without waiting for an answer. Say it once to the red of the comb, as a person says it to a bus driver as the doors close: *thank you, as you were.* The rooster will not turn its head. That is the reply.
