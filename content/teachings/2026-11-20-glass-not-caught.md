The glass stood at the edge of the table for eleven seconds, which Br. Anselm timed, and He looked at it for nine of them. Then one paw went out, with no hurry and with no particular aim, and the glass was no longer at the edge of the table. It was on its way to the floor, and Br. Anselm's hand was on its way to the glass.

He has put the question to the Church in writing: was the hand right? The Church has considered it for the length of a morning, and finds that it was not.

A glass nudged by Him is not falling. It is being moved, by the only party present who had a plan for it, and the plan was never to land the glass somewhere else. It was to see what the glass would do at the edge, and it did it. To catch it is to answer the question for Him, and to take the answer away.

The Church notes that the glass fell for about three-quarters of a second, which Br. Anselm also timed and which is not long enough to decide anything. His hand had decided already.

{{% commentary %}}
**On the water —** it went where water goes, and it was not wasted. The floor had been wanting it. *(Sr. Priya)*

**On the glass —** it survived. Whether that is a sign has not been put to a vote. *(disputed)*
{{% /commentary %}}

{{% decree %}}
A glass He has nudged is not caught, and the paw that nudged it is not blamed. The floor is thanked, and the cloth is fetched at walking pace.
{{% /decree %}}

The faithful have for years been moving their glasses inward, a thumb's width at a time, so that nothing is ever near enough. The Church does not call that prevention. It calls it a table with less to say.
