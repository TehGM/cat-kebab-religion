The hall at Sr. Rosalind's is eleven paces long, and at three in the morning He crossed it in four seconds. She was awake, which she puts down to the radiator, and she timed it from the bed with the watch she kept from her years in air traffic. Then she did what she was trained to do with a thing that moves fast through a controlled space and cannot be asked its intentions. She logged it as weather.

The circuit went hall, stairs, landing, the length of the landing, and the hall again. Nothing was touched on the way. The rug was not disturbed, which she checked in the morning, and a stack of post on the third stair was found exactly where she had left it, one envelope slightly more square than before. He went round once and stopped on the mat as though He had just got there, and the rest of the night was entirely quiet.

{{% testimony by="Sr. Rosalind" %}}
Wind, north-easterly, indoors. Gusting, for four seconds. Visibility nil. No damage, and no explanation requested.
{{% /testimony %}}

The Church holds that the circuit is the road taken inside a house, where the road is short and the kebab is the whole cat. It does not need a reason, and the faithful who have asked for one have been told that the reason was the hour. He is in transit at three in the morning as He is at noon, and there is simply less furniture awake to see it.

{{% commentary %}}
**On the waking —** nobody in the house was woken on purpose. Several were woken. The Church draws the line there. *(Sr. Rosalind)*

**On the post —** squared, not moved. The Church takes this as a courtesy. *(disputed)*
{{% /commentary %}}
