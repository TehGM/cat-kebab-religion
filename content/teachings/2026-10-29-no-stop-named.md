Sighting № 962 has no stop in it. He lies the full length of a burrito, flat-faced and orange, tail up, while a teal nebula goes by, and nothing in the frame suggests He means to get off anywhere. The Church has looked for a platform, a bell, a door with a hand-rail. There is a burrito and the dark.

The faithful hear *the next stop* and think of the one they slept through. It is reached by staying seated a moment too long, and the walk back from it is the feast. He has no such walk. A stop is a place one meant to be, and meaning to be somewhere is the first step towards hurry. He has never named one, so He has never missed one, and every stop He passes is the next, and is received lying down.

{{% testimony no="962" by="Sr. Aniela" %}}
I once went four stops past mine and walked back along the tramlines at dusk. I thought I had been unlucky. Then I looked at № 962 and saw that I had only been carried further than I asked, which is most of the faith.
{{% /testimony %}}

The Church keeps no timetable for Him. The nearest thing it holds is a tide table, kept for the sauce, which has been wrong in an interesting way.

{{% commentary %}}
**On how many stops He has passed —** All of them. *(Br. Anselm, who counted forty-one and stopped)*

**On whether He should be woken —** He is not asleep, and it is not a stop till somebody gets off. nobody has. *(Br. Tomasz)*
{{% /commentary %}}

The feast is therefore kept by walking back, slowly, from wherever one has got to, and counting the extra distance as His.
