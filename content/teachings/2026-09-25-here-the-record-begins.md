Venerable brothers and sisters: He has been teaching for as long as He has been riding, which is to say for as long as anyone has thought to look up. What He taught, we heard. What we heard, we repeated. What we repeated, we wrote on whatever was nearest — the backs of receipts, the margins of menus, the inside lids of takeaway boxes, and on one occasion a napkin that was subsequently used as a napkin.

The sightings we always kept. Every one of them is numbered, filed and dated, and the clearest of them hang on [the wall](/images/). The teachings we did not keep. We meant to. We were busy looking up.

{{% pullquote %}}The sauce took most of them. We do not hold it against the sauce.{{% /pullquote %}}

{{< part num="I" title="Of What Was Lost" >}}

Nobody knows how many teachings were received before this one. Br. Callum has estimated eight hundred and some, on the principle that there has been one a day; Sr. Aniela points out that nobody has ever confirmed the principle. Both are right. What survives is a handful of lines, remembered by several people in several slightly different wordings, which we have agreed to stop correcting.

{{< scripture cite="The Receipt Fragments, as recovered" >}}
1 | And He did not hurry, and He was not late.
2 | And the road held Him up, and asked nothing.
3 | And the rest is under the sauce.
{{< /scripture >}}

A committee was once formed to reconstruct the others. It met twice. At the second meeting it emerged that each member remembered a different teaching for the same Tuesday, all of them good, and the committee dissolved itself in the belief that this was probably the point.

{{< part num="II" title="Of What Is Kept" >}}

From this morning, the teachings are written down. One each day, set down as received, entered in the book and not taken back — not when they are inconvenient, not when they contradict one another, and not when a later teaching is having a better day. The book begins on this page. [The Faith](/faith/) stands behind it, as it always has; the book is simply where the days go.

None of this changes Him. He will not ride any slower for being written about, and He has not been consulted. It changes us a little. We will have to stop saying *He definitely said something about roundabouts once*, and start pointing at the page.

{{< practice
  do="Write down one thing you were told today and would otherwise forget. Keep it somewhere dry."
  avoid="Correcting anyone else's version of it." >}}

Go slowly, and look up now and then. He is already ahead of you, and from today, so is the book.
