One skewer goes through the kebab, and a second lies in the drawer beside the unsent letters. It is the same length, from the same box, and it has never once been needed. The faithful ask what it is for. The Church answers that it is for the day the first one gives out, that the day has not come, and that the skewer is not wasted by waiting.

A thing kept in case is already at work. It is being in case, which is a full-time position and a quiet one, and it does it without once being asked. Br. Anselm took it out of the drawer last spring to measure it, found it exactly as long as the other, and put it back before it could be thought of as in use. The Church counts that as the nearest it has come.

Nobody is to be sorry for it. A spare that is never called upon has not been overlooked, and it has not failed. It has simply been outlasted by the first skewer's good behaviour, and the kebab, which came through whole, is the spare's doing as much as anyone's.

Sr. Lucia has pointed out that He rides the same way. The helmet and the two small rockets in № 2,450 are worn for the look of the thing, and are not needed, and nobody is cross with them. The Church adds only that a spare is the one piece of equipment that is not worn at all, and so is the more modest.

On the day after, a second spare is laid in beside the first. Nothing has changed. The drawer is simply better prepared for the same nothing.
