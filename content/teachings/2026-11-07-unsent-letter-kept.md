There are thirty-one letters in the drawer that were never posted, and all thirty-one are addressed in the same hand to the flight, care of the weather. The Church did not plan this. Each writer, alone and at different times, arrived at the same line for the same reason, which is that nobody knows where He is, and everybody knows what the weather is doing.

The Church has read none of them. It has counted the envelopes, weighed the drawer, and found that it still shuts, which is as much as a drawer can be asked.

It is the custom to write one, and the custom is kept like this. Begin it as a letter to Him, and do not make it good. Say what you would say if the post were fast and He were slow, and it would arrive just as He passed. Then address it care of the weather, because the weather is the only party certain to be where He is going. Do not wait for the right weather. Nobody has found it, and the Church suspects that it is not a matter of finding.

A brother once asked whether a letter that is never sent has been written. The Church holds that it has. The first draft of the first teaching sits folded in the same drawer, sauce ring and all, and nobody doubts it was written. A letter that goes unsent is a letter that has not yet been interrupted.

{{% decree %}}
The letter is kept, not sent. The drawer will be opened when the weather is right, and not before.
{{% /decree %}}

The drawer has never yet been opened on a day the Church thought the weather was right, and it is not troubled by this. It is a long drawer.
