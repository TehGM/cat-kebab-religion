There is a ticket in the pocket of the coat you wore in March. It is for one journey, single, adult, and the journey was made. The ticket has been folded in half and then in half again, by a hand that did not decide to, and it has since gone soft at the creases like a thing that has been somewhere.

The Church has been asked what is to be done with it, and the answer is the same each time. It is not used again, because it is spent. It is not thrown away, because it is the only proof that the journey took place, and the Church has found that proof of this kind is very hard to come by afterwards. It is kept. That is all the keeping asks.

Br. Anselm has measured his, folded: eleven millimetres by nineteen, which he says is the size a ticket becomes when left to itself. He has not unfolded it to check the destination. He knows the destination. The Church takes this to be the whole of the devotion.

The Prophet carries no ticket. He is understood to have been on every journey already, and to need no proof of it. This is a different matter from the pocket, and the Church does not confuse the two, though it did once try to compile a list of tickets kept by the faithful, and gave up at forty-one, none of them to anywhere the Church had been.
