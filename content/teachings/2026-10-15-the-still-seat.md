The loop is eleven seconds long, and in all eleven He appears not to move. The burger does not move. The stars go by behind Him at their usual rate, and the faithful have been arguing for some time about what that means.

The First Party holds that He is travelling, as the Faith requires, and that this is simply what travelling looks like from the burger. A creature in transit on a double cheeseburger, they say, sits perfectly level while the sky is brought past, which is the more courteous arrangement. The Second Party agrees that He is in transit, since nobody has seen Him standing still, and holds that the sky is merely busy. He is moving, they say, so smoothly that the movement has gone into the stars. Nobody has asked the sky to stop, and it has not offered.

The Faith settles that He moves. It does not say which of the two of them is doing the work.

He moves, the sky is still | Disproved by the stars, which are visibly going somewhere.

Both move, at the same rate | Unfalsifiable, and held by Br. Dmitri.
The burger | Takes no side. Two tiers, both cheeses, level throughout.

{{% commentary %}}
**On the stars —** Eleven seconds, and not one of them was in a hurry. I timed it twice, and they were not. *(Br. Anselm, who measures things)*

**On the chart —** My chart says they are fixed. They have never once been where the chart says. *(Br. Callum, owner of a telescope)*

**On the question —** The Church declines to say who is moving, and notes that He has not looked up. *(Br. Dmitri)*
{{% /commentary %}}

The matter is retained. The Church observes only that a cat on a double cheeseburger, with the whole sky passing, has kept His dignity at the exact height at which He arrived, and that in eleven seconds no one in the photograph needed to know who was going where.
