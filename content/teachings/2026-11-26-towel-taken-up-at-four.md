The towel is laid on the kitchen floor at the near edge of the light, folded once, square to the skirting. This is done at about eleven, by whoever is up, and nobody has ever been asked to do it. The sunbeam then crosses the floor at the pace of the minute hand and goes out from under the towel by a quarter past, leaving the towel in the shade of its own good intentions. The faithful have been laying towels for as long as there have been beams. The beam has not yet been seen to mind.

The Church does not call this a mistake. A place kept in a thing that is leaving is still a place kept, and the towel is the one object in the kitchen that has been told where He will want to be and has gone there first. Sr. Maeve moves hers a paw's width at a time, by the clock, and says it is the only housework she has ever done in step with anything.

Why four? The Church has been asked, and says only that the light has left the floor by then, and that a towel kept down after it is no longer reserving anything. It is a rug. The Feast ends when the towel is lifted, and not a minute before, whatever the weather has done.

{{< litany label="Said at the lifting" >}}
The beam has gone up the wall | and He with it.
The towel was laid | and is taken up.
The floor is only a floor | and was warm.
{{< /litany >}}

In Br. Anselm's kitchen there is a paler square on the lino where the towel has lain every year, and nobody has asked which of the two was faded by the other. The Church has not looked into it, and suggests that it should not.
