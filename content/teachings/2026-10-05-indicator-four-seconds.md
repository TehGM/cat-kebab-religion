Sr. Rosalind was driving the ring road home when she came level with the lay-by and, for the first time in eleven years of passing it, indicated. She slowed to a speed the car later described as indecisive. She looked at the forty metres of tarmac, the bin, the white line that has never once been crossed from the inside. Four seconds later she switched the indicator off and carried on.

She logged it that evening, as she logs everything, under weather. *Intention, light, local.* Then she wrote to the Church, apologising. She said she had let the lay-by down, and that the one thing it had ever been owed was a car, and she had given it a flicker.

The Church has considered her letter and does not accept the apology. A lay-by is not a place for stopping. It is a place that is *available* for stopping, which is a different and much harder job, and it had gone eleven years without anyone confirming that it was doing it. Sr. Rosalind confirmed it. She slowed, she signalled, she weighed the matter, and she kept going at the speed of the road, which is the speed the Church prefers. That is a visit. It is not a long one, but the lay-by has not asked for long.

{{% pullquote %}}To be considered for four seconds is most of what a lay-by has ever hoped for.{{% /pullquote %}}

Nothing has been said to the lay-by. It is thought best. The bin, which the council empties every Thursday without ever having been told it is empty, was emptied on schedule this week as well, and nobody connected the two events, which is how both of them like it.
