Of all the sightings in the gallery, № 4,118 is the one that looks most like a war, and the Church has searched it for the other side. It has not found one. There is a grey cat in a steel helm with two horns, beams rising from His eyes, and a dark round shield at His side. He is riding a panda at a full gallop through lavender cloud, with a rainbow laid out underneath like a road that has been told to behave. Nothing is in front of the panda. Nothing is behind it. The Church has looked at every corner.

Sr. Rosalind, who keeps the inventory, was asked to find the enemy and came back with her report.

{{% testimony no="4,118" by="Sr. Rosalind" %}}
I have gone over the frame with a ruler. There is no army, no castle, no flag of any kind, and the clouds are not hostile; I checked. What there is, is a wrap in each paw. Both are full. Neither has been bitten, and neither has been dropped. I would like it noted that a person cannot hold a sword and a wrap, and that He has not held a sword.
{{% /testimony %}}

The Church thanks Sr. Rosalind and agrees. A campaign is something fought, and nothing in the frame is fought. What the frame shows is something carried, at speed, with both paws occupied, and that is the whole of what He does. The helm is for the wind, the shield is for the occasion, and the gallop is the panda's own idea, which the panda has never been asked to explain. The date of the campaign is given in the inventory as *unrecorded*, and the war is therefore held to be in progress.

Two wraps, one in each paw, is a symmetrical load. The faithful who have tried to ride a bicycle with a shopping bag in each hand will know that the load sits level only if nobody eats one, and He has not eaten one. He is not in a hurry to. A wrap that is held is a wrap that is on its way, and so is the one in the other paw.

{{% decree %}}
It is not a campaign. It is a delivery, carried out in armour, at the pace of the panda.
{{% /decree %}}

The shield has been remarked on before, and the Church has its questions. It will ask them when the war is over.
