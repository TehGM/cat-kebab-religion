The lid has a ring of sauce on the inside and a smudge on the outside that may be writing. That is the whole of the evidence. Sr. Rosalind has kept it for six years in the second drawer down, between a battery that may or may not work and a key to nothing.

She has not read it. She has held it to the window twice, once at noon and once at dusk, and both times the smudge declined to settle into a letter, a line or a word. She logged both as weather: clear, with sauce later.

The Church holds that a takeaway lid may carry a teaching, and that the sauce took most of them. It has never held that every lid does. The two positions meet in the drawer, and the Church has found that they can stay there for years, since a lid that has not been read is, until it is, both a fragment and a lid, and nobody is asked to choose. Read it, and it becomes one thing. The Church has noticed that this is nearly always the lid.

{{% commentary %}}
**On reading it —** At once, and with the lamp on. It is surely a teaching, and I will not sleep until we know. *(Br. Michał, disputed)*

**On not reading it —** It is surely a lid, and she would like to go on thinking otherwise. *(Br. Anselm, measuring the smudge at four millimetres)*

**On either —** it still counts. *(Br. Tomasz)*
{{% /commentary %}}

The ring on the inside is round, and the Church has read nothing into that. It is a ring, left by a thing that was warm, and it is kept with the rest.

So the lid stays, unread and unthrown, and the drawer is not to be tidied on its account. Sr. Rosalind opens it from time to time for something else, sees the lid, and closes the drawer again, gently, as one closes a door on a sleeping person. This is the whole of the observance. It has been kept since before it was named.
