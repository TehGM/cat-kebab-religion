The request came at five. It came from the doorway of the kitchen, in a voice that has been described as a hinge, and it was addressed to the bowl, to the cupboard and to Sr. Priya, in that order. Dinner was at six. It has been at six for as long as there has been a kitchen, and He knows this as well as the clock does, and asks regardless.

The faithful have since divided over what is being asked. Both parties keep the household's hour, and neither has been rude about it.

{{< doctrine >}}
The Party of the Clock | The hour is six. A meal asked for at five is a meal asked for early, and to give it early teaches the hour that it may be moved. The bowl is filled at six, and the voice is received as one receives weather.
The Party of the Voice | The hour is whenever He says it. A request made at volume is a request made, and the Church does not hold a clock above a voice. The bowl is filled at five, and six is merely when it is over.
{{< /doctrine >}}

Br. Dmitri has been on both sides within one week and defends each with equal force. He says it is the only honest position, since he served by the clock on Tuesday and by the voice on Thursday, and on neither day was it his dinner.

The Church has looked into what the hour is. It finds that it is a very short span. He asks at five, is not fed, asks again at ten past, and is fed at six, and nobody in the household can say that the five o'clock request did not make the six o'clock meal what it was. A meal asked for for an hour has been looked forward to by someone very capable of it, and that is not nothing.

{{% decree %}}
The bowl is filled at the hour, and the asking is entered as time well spent.
{{% /decree %}}

Neither party is rebuked. The Church observes only that in every house where this is argued, the bowl is filled by six.
