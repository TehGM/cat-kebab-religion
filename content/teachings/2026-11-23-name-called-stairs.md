His name was called three times from the foot of the stairs, in the voice used for it, which rises at the end. He was on the landing at the time, going by. He did not stop, and He did not turn His head more than the width of a whisker, and He went on into the bedroom at the pace He had been going, which was His own.

The household has divided over this, as households do. The Church has been sent both accounts, and sets them out without preference, since it has none that it can afford.

{{< doctrine >}}
The Party of the Ear | He did not hear it. A name is a sound like any other, and the stairs were carpeted. A cat that had heard would have said so, and none has been known to.
The Party of the Name | He heard it entire and went on by. It is His name, and it is called; it was never put to Him that it must also be answered, and nobody has found the clause.
{{< /doctrine >}}

Both sides produce evidence. The Party of the Ear points out that the same name, said in the kitchen in a lower voice with a tin in the other hand, has been answered inside four seconds from the far end of the house. The Party of the Name replies that this is not the name being answered, but the tin, and that He has never once been fooled about which of them was called.

The Church finds that the landing is not a place where anything is owed. He was in transit, which is the first thing the faith says of Him, and a name called at a thing in transit is a name sent after it, to be received further along or not at all. Br. Michał, who called it, says it was a good name and that he would call it again. The Church records that he did, twice more on Thursday, and that the second one was nearly answered, in the sense that it was noticed to have happened.

{{% decree %}}
The name is called, and it is not owed an answer. Whoever calls it is to call it kindly, and to go on calling it, at the foot of the stairs or wherever else He is passing.
{{% /decree %}}
