**What is the tide table?**

It is a sheet of figures pinned in the reading room, beside the clock, giving the hours at which the garlic sauce is high and the hours at which it is low. Sr. Rosalind drew it up from eleven weeks of observing the tub in the depot canteen. She logged the readings as weather, which is how she logs everything.

**Is it right?**

No. The sauce is high when the table says low, and low when it says high, and on most Thursdays it is neither.

**Then why does the Church keep it?**

Because it is wrong in an interesting way. The table gives twelve hours and twenty-five minutes between one high and the next, and the sauce, whenever it comes in, comes in at very nearly that interval. The table has the rhythm exactly and the hour entirely wrong. Sr. Rosalind says this is the more difficult half to get, and the Church takes her word, having never got either.

**Has anyone corrected it?**

Once. A brother moved every figure forward by four hours and ten minutes, and the sauce was observed to come in at the old times, on the old wall, for the length of one week. The sheet was put back. Nobody asked the sauce, and the Church does not think it would have said.

**Should the faithful plan their wraps by it?**

They should not. They should read it, and then have the wrap when they would have had it, and note the difference. The table is not for deciding anything. It is for being consulted.

**What is the consulting for?**

To have looked.

**And what does He make of it?**

Nothing is recorded. The Church holds that He keeps no tide table, being on the tide.
