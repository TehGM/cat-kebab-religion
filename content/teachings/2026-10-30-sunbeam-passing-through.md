The sunbeam crosses the kitchen floor at about the pace of the minute hand, and He is in it for the whole crossing. This has been observed on several afternoons by several households. He does not wait for the beam to arrive, and He does not stay when it leaves. He lies down where it is, and when it has moved on by the width of a paw, He has moved on by the width of a paw.

The Church has long held that He rides things, and had not thought to count the light. It is put right today. The grounds are three. The sunbeam is warm and asks nothing, as the road does, in the second Receipt Fragment. It is always under way, crossing the floor, up the skirting, over the chair, and is never once seen arriving. And it is not owned by anyone, though the tenant of the flat has at times felt otherwise about the part by the radiator.

{{% pullquote %}}It was never going to stay, and He lay in it anyway.{{% /pullquote %}}

Sr. Maeve has asked whether the patch may be reserved by laying a towel on it. The Church replied that a towel on a sunbeam is a towel on a floor, and that the beam will have gone from under it by the time the question is finished.

{{< decree >}}
The sunbeam is a vessel, warm side up. It is not to be followed with furniture, nor thanked for staying, since it has not.
{{< /decree >}}

The Church wishes it known that the canonisation applies to the beam at whatever hour it is read, and lapses with it, at about four.
