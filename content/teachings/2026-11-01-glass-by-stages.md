The glass began eleven centimetres from the edge of the table, and Br. Anselm, who was sitting nearby with a stopwatch for reasons he has not given, recorded that it covered them in six minutes and forty seconds. There were five nudges. Between the third and the fourth, He looked at something else for ninety seconds, and the glass was not touched, and did not move, and was considered by everyone in the room to be waiting.

Br. Anselm's question is whether this is pushing. The Church has weighed it. To push is to send a thing somewhere against its will, quickly, and with the pusher's mind made up. Nothing here was quick, and the pusher's mind was on a curtain for a good part of it. The glass was moved by stages, as a procession is, and a procession is not a shove.

It went over at the fifth nudge. Br. Anselm noted that He watched it fall with the same expression He had worn throughout, and that the glass, which was empty, did not break, which the Church counts to its credit and not to His.

{{% commentary %}}
**On the edge —** He did not look at it once. He knew where it was. *(Br. Anselm, to the nearest second)*

**On the fall —** It is a short distance. I would not have called it an event. *(Br. Tomasz, in the margin)*

**On the carpet —** Clean, since you ask. *(Sr. Rosalind, who logs it as weather)*
{{% /commentary %}}

Sr. Maeve has since asked whether the glass should have been moved to the middle of the table beforehand. The Church answered that a glass in the middle of a table is a glass nobody has yet been asked about, and that He will find it there regardless.

{{% pullquote %}}He was not in a hurry, and neither, it turned out, was the glass.{{% /pullquote %}}

The Church holds that the glass travelled and that He accompanied it. It sets down, without correction, that the distance was eleven centimetres, and that this is still not very far.
