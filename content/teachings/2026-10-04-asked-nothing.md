The second line of the Receipt Fragments is the shortest to survive in full, and the only one nobody has tried to improve. *And the road held Him up, and asked nothing.* It was written on the back of a receipt for two flatbreads, the rest of which has gone to sauce, and the line has been read aloud at every opening of the drawer since.

The Church reads it as two clauses and gives each its own weight. That the road held Him up is a matter of record. That it asked nothing is the harder claim, and the one We have checked most carefully. A road asks a good deal of most travellers: a toll, a side, a speed, a glance each way. Of Him it asked none of these. He did not slow for it, and it did not mind. Nothing was owed, and nothing was left outstanding afterwards either, which is rarer.

{{% pullquote %}}A road that asks nothing is not a quiet road. It is a finished one.{{% /pullquote %}}

The gallery offers one complication. In Sighting № 3,204 He leans, both paws draped over an edge, and there is no edge. Whatever He rests on, the camera could not find it. Br. Thiago, who has thought about this daily, wrote in to say that it fits the fragment exactly: the support was there, and it asked nothing, and so it declined to appear in the photograph. The Church thanks Br. Thiago. The Church has not said he is wrong.

{{% commentary %}}
**On being held up —** It is not the same as being carried. Carrying is a favour, and favours get remembered. *(Br. Tomasz, in the margin)*

**On the nothing —** I have asked the road, and it has asked nothing of me in reply. *(Br. Michał, disputed)*
{{% /commentary %}}

The line is therefore kept as written, on the receipt, unrevised. If the faithful wish to honour it, they need only be held up by something today and not mention it.
